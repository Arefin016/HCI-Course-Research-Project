
# Impact of a Gamified IDE on the Productivity of Programmers with ADHD

## Overview

This research explores how gamification within Integrated Development Environments (IDEs) can support the productivity, motivation, and focus of software developers with Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD).

The study proposes a gamified IDE plugin designed using Werbach and Hunter's 6D Gamification Framework to address common workplace challenges, including task inertia, time management difficulties, and maintaining focus on monotonous programming tasks.

## Research Questions

1. What specific productivity challenges do programmers with ADHD face while using standard IDEs?
2. How can gamified elements within an IDE promote better motivation and focus for programmers?

## Methodology

This study proposes a mixed-methods approach combining:

- Online surveys
- Semi-structured interviews
- IDE telemetry data
- Pre- and post-deployment evaluations

The proposed study includes a four-week deployment of the gamified IDE plugin with an initial target of 20–25 participants.

## Proposed Gamification Features

### 1. Nudges
Provides gentle notifications to help developers resume their previous tasks after distractions.

### 2. FocusPoints
Rewards developers with experience points (XP) for completing manageable tasks, making smaller commits, and initiating work promptly.

### 3. Unlock Goodies
Allows developers to redeem earned points for exclusive IDE themes and icon packs.

## Research Framework

The proposed plugin follows Werbach and Hunter's 6D Gamification Framework:

1. Define business objectives
2. Delineate target behaviors
3. Describe your players
4. Devise activity cycles
5. Don't forget the fun!
6. Deploy the appropriate tools

## Expected Contributions

This research aims to:

- Identify productivity challenges experienced by programmers with ADHD.
- Investigate the potential impact of gamification on motivation and focus.
- Explore accessible and inclusive IDE design.
- Provide design guidelines for supporting neurodivergent software developers.
